# Heat pump quote comparison — evidence and limitations
Reviewed 2026-11-03.

## Official sources

- https://www.portland.gov/ppd/residential-permitting/residential-mechanical-permits — mechanical permits for heat pumps and mini-splits; electrical permits needed for most projects; outdoor setback and final inspection relevance.
- https://www.portland.gov/ppd/residential-permitting/residential-electrical-permits — new or changed hardwired systems require electrical permits; final electrical inspection required.
- https://www.oregon.gov/energy/incentives/Pages/HP3.aspx — state HP3 program; general efficiency minimum HSPF2 >=8.1 and SEER2 >=16, existing owner-occupied $0 unreserved as of 2026-10-01, rental and new construction funds reported as available then (not current guarantee). Approved contractors provide incentives as upfront price reductions. HP3 is limited to eligible Oregon residential properties and has restrictions on stacking federally funded incentives.

## Conservative arithmetic

Quotes can be marked as before or after discount. After-discount quotes never have entered discounts subtracted twice. Before-discount quotes subtract a **user-entered** other discount only when marked confirmed. A user-entered HP3 proposed amount is included in a conditional arithmetic scenario ONLY for eligible Oregon property categories other than existing-owner-occupied, and only when user marks current funding/reservation, approved contractor, required equipment ratings, and upfront price deduction confirmed. Even then, **this is NOT proof of incentive award**, and the agency can reject an application.

Any missing or nonpositive HSPF2/SEER2 value prevents counting HP3 in the arithmetic. The tool flags missing permits, electrical scope, final inspection, warranties, and model documentation, never certifying their adequacy.

A ten-year 'installed price + annual operating cost' example is shown ONLY if user enters annual cost. No utility rate, bill savings, heat load, financial discount rate, maintenance, degradation, fuel price increases or tax credit assumptions are made. It is illustrative, not a forecast.

## Validation and release

1. Pure engine tests cover double subtraction, no rebates for owner-occupied snapshot, funding/contractor failures, permits, price comparisons, negative amounts and quote count constraints.
2. Confirm with actual Portland homeowners and licensed installers using only voluntarily entered *redacted* price information; no identifying contractor/customer data.
3. Audit program's most recent funding table, specific utility rebates, contractor approval and eligibility before adding new incentives or publicly releasing.
4. Use source registry and optional network probe only to flag review, not change rules.
5. Remain noindex and deployment-protected until human validation.
