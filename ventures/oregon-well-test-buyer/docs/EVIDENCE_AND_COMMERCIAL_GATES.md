# WellOrder — Regulatory evidence, safety scope and commercial falsification

**Primary-source review:** November 7, 2026. **Regulatory code expires in 30 calendar days without re-review.** Oregon private groundwater domestic-well households only. The tool is an independent buyer checklist, **not** a regulated laboratory, accredited sampler, drinking-water safety assessor, medical adviser, water-treatment installer or OHA filing agent.

## Primary sources and executable decisions

1. **[OHA routine testing recommendations](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/ph/healthyenvironments/drinkingwater/sourcewater/domesticwellsafety/pages/testing-recommendations.aspx)**: Oregon does not routinely mandate homeowner domestic-well testing outside real-estate sales (new-well construction separate). OHA recommends **annual** coliform/**E. coli** and **nitrate** testing, and **arsenic every 3–5 years**. Other parameters and more frequent tests may be appropriate for health, contamination and equipment conditions. OHA lists indicative costs of individual tests, but these figures **must not be presented as a merchant's live price**. Code treats arsenic at 3 years as conservative **recommended**, never a legal deadline.
2. **[OHA Well Testing & Real Estate Transactions](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/ph/healthyenvironments/drinkingwater/sourcewater/domesticwellsafety/pages/testing-regulations.aspx)** and **[ORS 448.271](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors448.html)**: Qualifying **seller** must test **arsenic, nitrate, total coliform** after accepting an offer and give buyer results and OHA RET form + results within **90 days** of receiving them; within-one-year compliant lab results may be reused. Follow collection rules **OAR 333-061-0335** and accredited laboratory testing **OAR 333-061-0330**. The app neither files nor asserts compliance. OHA says specified spring, irrigation-only and undeveloped-land well cases are exempt from **these property-sale rules**, not necessarily safe to consume untreated.
3. **[ORELAP accreditation search](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/ph/laboratoryservices/environmentallaboratoryaccreditation/pages/tni-lams.aspx)**: The public ODIE/Oregon Environmental Laboratory Accreditation Program search can reveal **individual lab, methods and analytes**, without a public user account. This app intentionally makes no real-time determination of accreditation status, nor claims a business is approved or open to public orders.
4. **[OHA lab listings and interactive map](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/ph/HealthyEnvironments/DrinkingWater/Monitoring/Pages/labs.aspx)**: A published lab map contains accredited providers and certain analytes, but OHA says **contact labs directly** for actual services. This app links to the official directory instead of pretending it maintains a verified merchant catalogue.
5. **[EPA private well guidance](https://www.epa.gov/privatewells/protect-your-homes-water)**: EPA also recommends **annual pH and total dissolved solids (TDS)** checks along with coliform and nitrate; they are a consumer discussion item for routine owners, **not** added as purported Oregon statutory sale tests.
6. **[ORELAP drinking-water testing](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/PH/LaboratoryServices/EnvironmentalLaboratoryAccreditation/Pages/DrinkingWaterTesting.aspx)**: Rules and methods evolve, including 2026 rulemaking. No assumption that a lab is accredited for *all* desired tests from its overall name or from a past certificate.

## Avoiding unnecessary orders

A test is neither inferred current nor due from an unverified memory. For each requested analyte, visitors may choose **never**, **unknown**, or **dated report**. Unknown dates block specific purchase recommendations until found. Valid original sampling dates are evaluated against a **one-year calendar anniversary** for sale and nitrate/coliform routine checks, or a conservative **three-year anniversary** for arsenic recommendation. A recent sale test is considered reusable only after visitor verifies appropriate past accreditation and legally compliant sampling. Nonrepeat paths **do not waive seller reporting** or certify water safety.

An unexplained flood, detected contamination, affected area, new-well job or other condition cannot be solved with a fixed three-analyte package. Visitors should contact OHA/county for individualized testing and interim water-use guidance. The product **does not collect water test numeric values**, so it cannot provide a safe-to-drink diagnosis and does not route people to filtration sellers.

## Purchase comparison rules

The app compares **two written lab quotes supplied by the homeowner**, never advertised prices scraped from unrelated lab pages. Each quote records scope, method-specific accreditation *confirmed by the visitor*, acceptance of public samples, proper containers and holding times, turnaround, same delivery method, base price, delivery/collection costs, all mandatory extra fees and Oregon property-sale sampling compliance where relevant. Unknown fees never mean zero; all-in first-order totals only compare if **both** are complete. No lab selection, provider endorsement, binding fee, referral or payment link.

The app contains no external API call, local persistence, address, legal identity, laboratory order number, homeowner health diagnosis or server-stored price. It offers only client-side copy and downloadable text for independently contacting accredited labs.

## Commercial thesis: HOLD until evidence

This is a different buying context (accredited environmental laboratories) from electrical contractor quotes, Arizona licensing or cruise activities. It could offer a referral or paid-test-ordering workflow if authorized laboratories actually accept qualified, preselected consumer orders. But note the economics: these are often **small laboratory transactions**, with individual OHA example tests listed in the tens of dollars. Even a functioning traffic channel may not support meaningful commissions without viable order volume, repeat purchasing, higher-value testing bundles or legitimate downstream services.

**Nothing verified:** partner acceptance, actual laboratory commission program, conversion rate, purchase capacity, exclusive leads, consumer willingness to pay for a planner, direct purchase integration, live test price, contractor referral rights, traffic or revenue. Zero commissions are activated. No third-party prices or laboratory business names are published as recommendations.

### Required prelaunch proof gates

- Interview at least 10 Oregon well-owning buyers and relevant ORELAP-accredited labs about real purchase friction, paperwork and referral permission before seeking a public indexable site.
- Confirm a *specific* lab supports the required sampling, analytes and lawful order/referral workflow; obtain contract and clear consumer fee disclosures. Do not imply the state endorses a provider.
- Have an Oregon real-estate / environmental compliance expert review OAR applicability to shared off-property wells, exempt cases, reuse and whether any designated-area extra tests apply.
- Test order-value economics and acquisition before building an expansive seller marketplace. An adverse result should end the experiment, not justify adding features.
- Re-review OHA/ORELAP sources within 30 days; run real desktop/mobile browsers against exact commit; explicit owner approval is required for any public launch, indexing or lead capture.

## Documentation and safety gate

No health-sensitive household composition, disability, pregnancy status, infant details, numeric lab readings or exact street addresses are requested. The app cannot declare water safe. If health or immediate contamination concerns exist, the result **fails closed** for tailored public-health advice. Legal consequences and consumer safety always take precedence over an unnecessary commercial referral.
