# WellOrder project boundaries

- Independent Oregon domestic-well laboratory purchasing project only; do not modify the parent Home Improvement app or inherit its shopper analytics, domains or commercial stack.
- OHA routine recommendations: nitrate and coliform/E. coli annually, arsenic about every 3–5 years. This is **not** the same as the mandatory **Oregon seller** arsenic/nitrate/total-coliform package triggered by an accepted offer under ORS 448.271.
- Protect no-buy paths: unknown sample history is not never-tested; legally reusable within-one-year seller tests require evidence of current dates, accredited analysis and correct sample collection; buyers do not automatically have the seller's statutory purchasing obligation. Never imply reporting is waived.
- If known flood/contamination, public-health area concern, new well, spring, municipal supply or other unsupported situations, **fail closed** and refer to OHA/county as appropriate. Do not offer water safety assessments, diagnosis or treatments without a separately reviewed professional workflow.
- Lab quotes and fees are **visitor-entered**, not live advertised prices. Only compare two same-scope/same-sampling-route proposals with complete analytes, methods and accreditation, packaging, holding-time confirmation, turnaround, all mandatory fees and sale-specific collection checks. Unknown is never zero.
- Sources manually reviewed 2026-11-07 and expire after 30 days. Source expiry must stop purchase recommendations and laboratory ranking. Do not independently claim state laboratory accreditation.
- No personal health details, water test chemistry results, addresses, email forms, lead submission, affiliate URLs, payment links, external fetches or user-specific analytics. No published or approved laboratory partners.
- All deployments require Vercel SSO **all** and noindex/robots blocking. Never merge or publicly launch unless owner authorizes it.
- Maintain independently passing Node unit tests, strict TypeScript and real Playwright Chromium desktop/mobile acceptance. Test discovery alone is insufficient.
